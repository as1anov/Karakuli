Ornament,

Hangers, shelves and magazine holders in steel. From ink on paper to metal: a path from the light to the lasting.

Scroll

Stone remembers. Geometry carved to last for centuries.

In the old buildings of Azerbaijan, geometry is carved right into the stone. Nameless masters left patterns on the walls that have outlived the centuries. There are no random lines in them: every turn is measured, every rhythm thought through.

Standing before such a wall, you understand that ornament can be eternal. This stone geometry became my first school of strictness and beauty.

The carpet: ornament that keeps the warmth of the hearth.

If stone speaks of eternity, the carpet speaks of the hearth. Azerbaijani carpets keep ornament in warm threads: diamonds, medallions, a rhythm with no beginning and no end.

You can look at a carpet for hours and find a new detail every time. That is what I wanted to learn: to make a pattern you want to come back to.

Shebeke: light gathered into a pattern.

Thousands of tiny wooden pieces lock into a lattice, and coloured glass flares between them. By day the window draws coloured ornaments on the floor, and the room comes alive.

What amazes me in shebeke is the master's patience, and how a strict structure gives birth to real magic.

From a free line to an ornament of my own.

At first the lines were born freely, by hand, alive and uneven. Then I began to look for their order. Out of scattered strokes came a form, out of the form came a rhythm.

Page by page, chaos gave way to harmony, but never vanished: it stayed in the pattern's character, in its liveliness. That is how the ornament I call Karakuli was born.

The geometry of choice.

A diamond is a sign. A line is movement. A circle is the sun, and a choice.

The circle is the sun, and eight directions, eight possible paths, lead out of it. The sun lights each of them, but does not decide where to go. The choice stays with you.

Karakuli is a geometry of choice, where every element has its meaning and every path stays open.
